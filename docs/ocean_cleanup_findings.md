### Critical Finding from Mani et al. (2026) — "Three Rivers to the Test"

**Key data points:**
- Studied 3 rivers: Rio Ozama (Dominican Republic), Umgeni (South Africa), Chao Phraya (Thailand)
- Traced **196 GPS drifters** across multiple seasons
- Deployed **41 cameras** at 6 river locations
- Simulated 3 years of plastic transport (2020–2022)
- **Rivers flush 50% of their plastics downstream within only 7–12% of the time** (event-driven)
- **Annual average mass fluxes were 34–98% LOWER than earlier global model estimates** (i.e., Meijer et al. 2021 overestimates)
- "Rivers are long-term pollution sinks, and estuaries have limited transport"
- This directly challenges the Meijer et al. (2021) emission estimates used for site selection

### Meijer et al. (2021) — The Foundational Model

**Key data points:**
- **>1,000 rivers account for 80% of global riverine plastic emissions**
- Global annual emissions range: **0.8–2.7 million metric tons per year**
- Small urban rivers are among the most polluting
- Uses probabilistic model: geographically distributed data on plastic waste, land use, wind, precipitation, rivers
- Calculates probability for plastic waste to reach a river and subsequently the ocean
- **Data available on figshare**: https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/14515590
- Point file contains locations of rivers and their modeled plastic emissions
- This model is THE basis for TOC's site selection and the "Top 1000 Polluting Rivers" interactive map

### Lebreton et al. (2017) — Earlier Model

**Key data points:**
- Estimated **1.15–2.41 million tonnes** of plastic waste enters the ocean from rivers per year
- **Top 20 polluting rivers** (mostly in Asia) account for **67% of the global total**
- **>74% of emissions occur between May and October** (seasonal)
- Calibrated against measurements available in the literature

## 2. THE 30 CITIES PROGRAM

### What It Is
- Announced June 12, 2025 at UN Ocean Conference (UNOC) in Nice
- Goal: **Stop up to 1/3 of global plastic emissions from rivers by 2030**
- Shifts from single-river deployments to **city-wide solutions** (multiple waterways per city)
- Built on learnings from Kingston, Jamaica (first "Interceptor City")
- **$121M committed by The Audacious Project** (TED's collaborative funding initiative) out of $350M needed
- Total program cost: **$350 million**

### How It Uses Meijer Estimates
- TOC used "the latest scientific modeling" (i.e., Meijer et al. 2021) to identify **30 major plastic-polluting coastal cities**
- The "Top 1000 Polluting Rivers" model (Meijer et al.) underpins site selection
- However, Mani et al. (2026) now shows actual fluxes are **34–98% lower** than Meijer estimates — this creates a significant discrepancy between modeled and observed emissions for the 30 Cities target rivers
- Each city undergoes **Smart River Survey** before deployment for ground-truthing

## 4. RIVER MONITORING DATASETS

### River Monitoring System (RMS)
- Bridge-mounted cameras that capture images of plastic debris in rivers
- Images used to train AI models to identify and categorize plastic pollution
- Pictures taken **every 15 minutes**, each picture 0.25 seconds apart (to measure debris speed)
- AI modeling identifies debris, measures size, differentiates organic vs. non-organic
- Deployed on 3 rivers (Umgeni, Chao Phraya, Rio Ozama) as part of the "Tale of 3 Rivers" study
- **23 cameras deployed in Thailand alone**; one deployment features 13 cameras covering 50% of the 260m-wide river cross-section
- **41 cameras total across 6 river locations** (per Mani et al. 2026)
- **No public data repository found** for RMS data

### Smart River Survey
- Created as part of the 30 Cities Program for rapid, replicable city assessments
- Uses: drones, AI-enhanced remote-sensing cameras, GPS drifters, global data registries
- Produces **interactive maps** for decision-makers showing pollution metrics and optimal deployment sites
- First process implemented when entering a new location
- Led by Renata Correia (Head of River Analysis & Performance)
- **No public data repository found** for Smart River Survey data

### GPS Drifters
- Grapefruit-sized floating GPS drifters report position every ~5 minutes to ~4m accuracy
- Used to trace plastic pathways (center vs. riverbank flow)
- **196 drifters** deployed across 3 rivers (Mani et al. 2026)
- Released in seasonal batches covering entire river life cycles
- Data used to optimize Interceptor placement
- **No public data repository found** for drifter data

### Figshare Dataset (Meijer et al. 2021)
- **Available**: https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/Supplementary_data_for_More_than_1000_rivers_account_for_80_of_global_riverine_plsatic_emissions_into_the_ocean_/14515590

## 6. KEY TAKEAWAYS & DATA GAPS

### What IS Available
1. **Meijer et al. (2021) global model dataset** on figshare — modeled emissions per river worldwide
2. **100+ peer-reviewed publications** listed at https://theoceancleanup.com/scientific-publications/
3. **Total catch numbers** (cumulative, DNV-verified) on the Dashboard
4. **Mani et al. (2026)** — the most important recent paper challenging global model estimates

### Critical Data Gaps
1. **No per-Interceptor capture efficiency data** published (what % of river plastic flux is actually captured)
2. **No per-river catch data** published in structured form (only aggregate totals)
3. **No RMS/Smart River Survey raw data** in public repositories
4. **No GPS drifter data** in public repositories
5. **34–98% discrepancy** between Meijer model estimates and Mani et al. (2026) observed fluxes — this means the "Top 1000 Polluting Rivers" rankings may significantly overestimate emissions
6. **30 Cities capture targets** are based on Meijer estimates which may overstate achievable impact by up to ~98% for some rivers
7. **No published analysis** of how the Mani et al. findings should revise the 30 Cities Program targets

### The Meijer-to-Reality Gap
The single most important finding for anyone using TOC data:

> "Annual average mass fluxes for the three rivers were **34–98% lower** than earlier global model estimates." — Mani et al. (2026)

This means:
- The Meijer et al. (2021) model (used for the "Top 1000 Rivers" map and 30 Cities site selection) likely **overestimates** river plastic emissions
- The ranking of rivers by emission may be directionally correct (polluting rivers are still polluting) but the **absolute magnitudes are unreliable**
- The "0.8–2.7 million metric tons/year" global estimate range may need significant downward revision
- Any modeling that uses Meijer estimates as input should treat them as **upper bounds** rather than central estimates
