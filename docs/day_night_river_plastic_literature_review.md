# Day-Night Differences in River Plastic Flux/Transport: Literature Review

## Summary

**The literature on day-night differences in river plastic flux is extremely sparse.** Only one published paper has explicitly measured both daytime and nighttime plastic transport in rivers. Several other papers acknowledge the night-time measurement gap as a major limitation. No paper has published a quantitative day:night ratio for river plastic flux.

---

## Papers with Actual Day + Night Measurements

### 1. Schreyers et al. (2024) - THE KEY PAPER

- **Full citation**: Schreyers, L. J., van Emmerik, T. H. M., Bui, T.-K. L., van Thi, K. L., Vermeulen, B., Nguyen, H.-Q., Wallerstein, N., Uijlenhoet, R., & van der Ploeg, M. (2024). River plastic transport affected by tidal dynamics. *Hydrology and Earth System Sciences*, 28(3), 589–610.
- **DOI**: 10.5194/hess-28-589-2024
- **River studied**: Saigon River, Vietnam (Thu Thiem bridge, Ho Chi Minh City)
- **Measured both day and night?**: YES - This is the first and, to our knowledge, only published study to explicitly measure river plastic flux during both day and night.
- **Method**: Visual counting from bridge (González-Fernández & Hanke, 2017 method). At night, a 4500 lumen flashlight (Ledlenser P18R Signature) was used to illuminate the water surface. 5 observation points across river width.
- **Duration**: Continuous 74.5 hours (1-4 May 2022), covering 6 full tidal cycles
- **Day:night ratio**: NOT EXPLICITLY REPORTED. The paper focuses on tidal (ebb/flood) dynamics rather than day/night comparisons. The authors note that "the difficulty in conducting measurements at night (due to the lack of daylight) and the resource-intensive nature of continuous measurements likely explain why such measurements have not been done thus far."
- **Key findings on temporal patterns**:
  - Net plastic transport is 20-33% of total plastic transport (rest is bidirectional tidal oscillation)
  - Plastic transport and river discharge are positively correlated (Pearson's R² = 0.76)
  - Soft/neutrally buoyant items had lower net transport (10-16%) than rigid/highly buoyant items (30-38%)
  - The paper does NOT separate day vs. night flux values in their results
- **Critical quote**: "The difficulty in conducting measurements at night (due to the lack of daylight) and the resource-intensive nature of continuous measurements likely explain why such measurements have not been done thus far."

## Papers Acknowledging the Day-Night Gap (but without night data)

### 2. van Emmerik & Schwarz (2019) - Review Paper

- **Full citation**: van Emmerik, T., & Schwarz, A. (2019). Plastic debris in rivers. *WIREs Water*, 7(1), e1398.
- **DOI**: 10.1002/wat2.1398
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - review paper
- **Relevance**: Identifies temporal variability (including diurnal) as a key knowledge gap in river plastic transport. Notes that most observations are limited to daytime visual surveys.

### 3. Vriend et al. (2025) - Uncertainties Paper

- **Full citation**: Vriend, P., Bosker, T., Mellink, Y., Collas, F., Moscoso Cruz, F., Kamp, N., Drok, S., Vijver, M. G., & van Emmerik, T. H. M. (2025). Uncertainties in Visual Observations of Floating Riverine Plastic. *ACS ES&T Water*, 5(7), 3920–3928.
- **DOI**: 10.1021/acsestwater.5c00223
- **River studied**: Dutch Rhine-Meuse delta
- **Measured both day and night?**: No
- **Key contribution**: Quantifies uncertainties in visual counting method, including observation time and frequency as key sources of uncertainty. Does not specifically address day vs. night but highlights that observation design (when and how long to observe) significantly impacts estimated flux.

### 4. van Emmerik et al. (2022b) - Roadmap for Monitoring

- **Full citation**: van Emmerik, T., Vriend, P., & Copius Peereboom, E. (2022). Roadmap for Long-Term Macroplastic Monitoring in Rivers. *Frontiers in Environmental Science*, 9, 802245.
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fenvs.2021.802245
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - framework paper
- **Relevance**: Provides roadmap for long-term monitoring, identifies need for continuous/24h monitoring but does not implement it.

### 5. Hurley et al. (2023) - Methods Harmonisation

- **Full citation**: Hurley, R., Veiteberg Braaten, H. F., Nizzetto, L., Steindal, E. H., & Moger, A. (2023). Measuring riverine macroplastic: Methods, harmonisation, and quality control. *Water Research*, 235, 119902.
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.watres.2023.119902
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - methods paper
- **Relevance**: Discusses quality control for river plastic monitoring. Implicitly acknowledges that visual methods are limited to daylight hours.

## Papers with Continuous/Subdaily Monitoring (but not analyzing day vs. night)

### 6. Schreyers et al. (2024b) - Water Hyacinths

- **Full citation**: Schreyers, L. J., van Emmerik, T. H. M., Bui, T.-K. L., Biermann, L., Uijlenhoet, R., Nguyen, H. Q., Wallerstein, N., & van der Ploeg, M. (2024). Water hyacinths retain river plastics. *Environmental Pollution*, 356, 124118.
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.envpol.2024.124118
- **River studied**: Saigon River, Vietnam
- **Measured both day and night?**: Unclear - likely daytime visual counts
- **Key finding**: Water hyacinth patches trap 54-77% of all observed surface plastics. Length scale of trapped plastics (~4.0 m) was 40x larger than open water plastics (~0.1 m).

### 7. van Emmerik et al. (2023) - Mekong Wet/Dry Season

- **Full citation**: van Emmerik, T., Schreyers, L. J., Mellink, Y. A. M., Sok, T., & Arias, M. E. (2023). Large variation in Mekong river plastic transport between wet and dry season. *Frontiers in Environmental Science*, 11, 1173946.
- **DOI**: 10.3389/fenvs.2023.1173946
- **River studied**: Mekong-Tonlé Sap-Bassac river system, Cambodia
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - daytime visual counts and net sampling
- **Key finding**: Wet season plastic transport up to 170x (items) and 294x (mass) higher than dry season. Harmonization approach for combining net and visual counting data.

### 8. Gnann et al. (2026) - Rhine 16-Month Continuous

- **Full citation**: Gnann, N., Höreth, K., Schweigert, N., Evers, M., et al. (2026). The river Rhine transports around 4,000 tonnes of macrolitter towards the North Sea each year. *Communications Earth & Environment*.
- **DOI**: 10.1038/s44458-025-00007-5
- **River studied**: Rhine River (16-month continuous monitoring)
- **Measured both day and night?**: Physical interception litter trap (24h passive collection) - so YES, both day and night, but trap is passive so no day/night separation
- **Method**: Custom-made physical-interception litter trap (passive) + citizen scientist sorting
- **Key finding**: 20,339 items collected over 16 months. Macrolitter abundance fluctuated by factor of 41 between biweekly samples. Higher at rising discharge. Annual estimate: 27-42.2 million items or 3,010-4,707 tonnes. **This is continuous but does not separate day from night flux.**

### 9. van Emmerik et al. (2023b) - Harmonized Monitoring

- **Full citation**: van Emmerik, T., Kirschke, S., Schreyers, L. J., Nath, S., Schmidt, C., & Wendt-Potthoff, K. (2023). Estimating plastic pollution in rivers through harmonized monitoring strategies. *Marine Pollution Bulletin*, 196, 115503.
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.marpolbul.2023.115503
- **River studied**: Mekong system, Cambodia
- **Measured both day and night?**: No
- **Relevance**: Provides harmonization framework. Does not address day-night issue.

### 10. van Waterschoot et al. (2026) - Estuary Mass Balance

- **Full citation**: van Waterschoot, D. M. P., Tas, S. A. J., Gratiot, N., Vriend, P., et al. (2026). Estimating plastic export from estuaries into the sea using an estuarine-mass-balance model. *Marine Pollution Bulletin*, 119793.
- **DOI**: 10.1016/j.marpolbul.2026.119793
- **Rivers studied**: Rhine-Meuse Estuary and Saigon Estuary
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - modelling study
- **Key finding**: Daily plastic export: 290 kg (Rhine-Meuse wet season), 19,431 kg (Saigon wet season). Deposition/remobilization from riverbanks = largest model fluxes. Most sensitive to deposition parameters.

### 11. Oswald et al. (2026) - Monitoring Methods Evaluation

- **Full citation**: Oswald, S. B., Vriend, P., Ragas, A. M. J., Schoor, M. M., et al. (2026). Evaluation of riverine macro- and mesoplastic monitoring approaches. *Environmental Monitoring and Assessment*.
- **DOI**: 10.1007/s10661-025-14889-4
- **Rivers studied**: Rhine-Waal Rivers
- **Measured both day and night?**: No - compared larvae net, trawl net, stow net
- **Relevance**: SWOT analysis of different monitoring methods; does not address day-night.

## Papers from The Ocean Cleanup

The Ocean Cleanup's river plastic work (Interceptors) uses passive collection systems that operate 24h. However, published scientific papers from The Ocean Cleanup on river plastic monitoring focus on global modelling (e.g., Meijer et al. 2021) rather than 24h flux analysis. The Interceptors collect continuously but published data has not been analyzed for day-night patterns.

### 12. Meijer et al. (2021) - Global River Model

- **Full citation**: Meijer, L. J. J., van Emmerik, T., van der Ent, R., Schmidt, C., & Lebreton, L. (2021). More than 1000 rivers account for 80% of global riverine plastic emissions into the ocean. *Science Advances*, 7(18), eaaz5803.
- **DOI**: 10.1126/sciadv.aaz5803
- **Relevance**: Global model; does not consider day-night variation in plastic flux.

## van Emmerik Group Publications Summary

The van Emmerik group (Wageningen University) is the most prolific group working on river plastic flux monitoring. Key members include Tim van Emmerik, Louise Schreyers, Paul Vriend, and Yvette Mellink. Their published works on the Saigon River are the closest to having day-night data:

| Paper | Year | River | Night data? | Method |
|-------|------|-------|-------------|--------|
| Schreyers et al. (HESS) | 2024 | Saigon | YES (flashlight) | Visual counting from bridge, 74.5h continuous |
| Schreyers et al. (Environ. Pollut.) | 2024 | Saigon | Likely no | Visual counting + camera |
| van Emmerik et al. (Front. Environ. Sci.) | 2023 | Mekong | No | Visual counting + net sampling |
| van Emmerik et al. (Mar. Pollut. Bull.) | 2023 | Mekong | No | Net + visual harmonization |

## Key Knowledge Gaps

1. **No published day:night ratio for river plastic flux** - Even Schreyers et al. (2024), which measured at night, does not report day vs. night transport separately
2. **Nighttime detection limitations** - Visual counting (the dominant method) requires daylight; flashlight method is used but its detection efficiency vs. daylight is unknown
3. **No systematic comparison** of daytime-only vs. 24h monitoring data
4. **Passive traps (e.g., Gnann et al. 2026)** collect 24h but cannot separate day/night contributions
5. **Camera-based automated systems** are being developed but are not yet validated for nighttime use
6. **Theoretical reasons to expect day-night differences**:
   - Diurnal variation in waste disposal patterns (more littering during daytime)
   - Diurnal rainfall patterns in tropical regions
   - Tidal cycles not aligned with day-night cycle (so day-night and tidal variation would be orthogonal)
   - Diurnal wind patterns affecting floating plastic transport
